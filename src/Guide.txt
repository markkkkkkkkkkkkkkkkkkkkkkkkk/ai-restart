AI RESTART
==========

Siri AI (the new Siri from Apple Intelligence) on a Mac whose system language it
does not support — Russian, Polish, Czech and many others.

Siri AI only turns on if the Mac BOOTED with English as the system language. You can
switch your language back afterwards and Siri AI keeps working until the next restart.
AI Restart does all of that by itself:
  1. Click the menu bar icon → "Restart…".
  2. It quits your apps, switches the system to English and restarts the Mac.
  3. For 20–60 seconds after login everything is in English: the system is bringing
     Siri AI up.
  4. Then your language comes back by itself and your apps reopen in it.
     You get an "Enhanced Siri is ready" notification.

There is no way to turn Siri AI on without a restart: macOS decides it only at boot.


WHAT YOU NEED
-------------
  • An Apple Silicon Mac (M1 or newer) with macOS 27 or newer.
  • Siri language: English (United States) — System Settings → Apple Intelligence & Siri.
  Nothing else: the app has no dependencies and downloads nothing.


INSTALLATION
------------
  1. Open the DMG and drag "AI Restart" to Applications.
  2. Launch it from Applications. The first time, macOS says it cannot verify the
     developer: System Settings → Privacy & Security → "Open Anyway" at the bottom.
  3. That's it. The AI Restart icon appears on the right of the menu bar — everything
     happens from there. The app added itself to login items; it has no Dock icon,
     that's on purpose.

  If the system is in English right now, the app asks which language to return to.
  To update: replace the app in Applications with the new one and launch it.

  If you don't see the icon, the menu bar is full and macOS hides the extras behind
  "«". Remove something you don't need, or drag icons while holding Cmd.


FIRST SETUP (if Siri AI has never arrived)
------------------------------------------
  The menu has a "First Setup" item that walks you through it and opens the right
  settings. Once Apple Intelligence has reached your Mac, the item disappears from
  the main menu and stays in "Settings". In short:

    1. System Settings → General → Language & Region: English (United States) first.
       Set the Siri language to English (United States) there as well.
    2. Restart the normal way, from the Apple menu. By hand, on purpose: the Apple
       Intelligence waitlist only opens on an English system, and that is decided
       at boot, when the app is not running yet.
    3. Install AI Restart (it asks which language to return to) and turn on Apple
       Intelligence: System Settings → Apple Intelligence & Siri.
    4. Right after that: menu bar icon → "Restore Language…", no restart needed.
       The waitlist and the model download continue in your own language (tested
       on two Macs).
    5. Until Siri AI arrives, restart only through AI Restart. The wait is anything
       from a few hours to a day.


HOW TO USE IT
-------------
  Click the AI Restart icon in the menu bar:

    Restart…              a window with a countdown, like the system one
    Shut Down…            the same, but the Mac shuts down
    Connect ChatGPT…      only appears while ChatGPT is not connected
    Restore Language…     only appears while the system is in English
    First Setup           step-by-step help; disappears once Apple Intelligence has
                          reached this Mac — after that it lives in "Settings"
    Settings              first setup, battery percentage in the menu bar, icon style,
                          low battery threshold and uninstalling the app
    Instructions          this file

  • While the menu is open, ⌘R (restart) and ⌘Q (shut down) work.
  • Restart and shut down the Mac ONLY from here. A regular restart from the Apple
    menu works, but Siri AI stays the old one until you use AI Restart again.
  • The window has only its own action, "Cancel" and the "Reopen windows" checkbox.
    Like the system window, it fires by itself after 60 seconds.
  • "Shut Down…" replaces shutting down from the Apple menu: on the next boot Siri AI
    comes up just like after a restart.
  • The "Reopen windows" checkbox brings your apps back in the same order with the
    same windows. Unchecked, you get a clean desktop.
  • If some app refuses to quit (waiting for you to save a document, say), the restart
    is cancelled with a message. Save the document and click again.


BATTERY PERCENTAGE IN THE MENU BAR
----------------------------------
  Settings → "Battery Percentage in Menu Bar". You get the indicator back: a number
  with a battery next to it. Since macOS 27 the system draws the percentage INSIDE
  the icon, and no system setting brings it back out — Control Center only has a
  "show percentage or not" switch.

  So the app draws the indicator itself: the number and the battery in a single icon,
  the way it used to be. A separate icon next to the system battery doesn't work —
  the menu bar keeps its own padding between two icons, and the gap comes out three
  times wider than it was. The system battery hides while this is on and comes back
  when you turn the item off or uninstall AI Restart. Switching restarts Control
  Center for a second, so the icons blink.

  The number uses fixed-width digits, like the system clock, so the battery doesn't
  jump around as the percentage changes.

  The icon is built from the system's own assets: the images Control Center uses to
  draw the battery live inside it, and the app takes the nub, the charging bolt and
  its mask from there — the same large bolt variant the system uses for a body this
  size. In macOS 27 the body itself is drawn as a fill rather than an image, so that
  is the only part drawn by hand, sized from that same asset. That's why the icon is
  indistinguishable from the system one. It takes its color from the menu bar like
  every other icon and follows light and dark themes. The fill follows the charge
  level, the bolt is knocked out of it while charging, it's yellow in Low Power Mode
  and red on a low battery (threshold configurable, see below).

  In the background the app does nothing and never wakes up on a timer: the system
  tells it when the percentage changed, when the charger was plugged or unplugged,
  when Low Power Mode was switched — and the icon redraws in the same second as the
  system one. Everything that needs other programs to run (time remaining, capacity,
  who is using the energy) is computed only when you open the menu.

  Clicking the indicator shows what the system battery used to show:
    • how much is left (or how long until fully charged);
    • battery capacity and cycle count — the same numbers as in Settings → Battery;
    • which apps use the most energy, with their icons, the hungriest on top. The
      measurement takes a second and a half, so the list is added to the menu that
      is already open. It's the "energy impact" Activity Monitor shows: not just the
      processor, but also wakeups from sleep, the GPU and the disk. Apps are counted
      together with their helper processes (Safari tabs, renderers and so on);
      services and background agents don't make the list. There is one difference
      from the system menu: it lists apps that use a lot of energy CONSTANTLY — the
      system averages over eight hours and keeps its threshold to itself. Here it's
      whoever is spending right now, at the moment you opened the menu;
    • "Low Power Mode and Settings…" opens Settings → Battery. The app cannot turn
      Low Power Mode on directly: pmset only changes it as an administrator, and the
      app will not ask for your password.

  Settings → "Icon Style": two versions of the same icon — "Like macOS 27" (a solid
  body, like the current system one) and "Like macOS 26" (a thin outline with the
  fill inside it, the way it was before). The size, the position and the placement of
  the number are identical down to the pixel; only the body drawing changes.

  Settings → "Low Battery": at which percentage the icon turns red and a warning
  arrives — 5, 10, 15 or 20 %. The default is 10 %, same as the system warning. The
  app has its own because the system threshold cannot be changed. The warning comes
  once per discharge: again only after the charge rises above the threshold or you
  plug the charger in. If Low Power Mode is already on, it doesn't come at all. The
  app cannot turn Low Power Mode on for you: pmset only changes it as an
  administrator and the app will not ask for your password — so the warning tells
  you where the switch is.

  Settings → "Percentage Opens AI Restart Menu": then the app doesn't need an icon of
  its own, the menu opens when you click the number, and the battery details move to
  a "Battery" submenu. In a crowded menu bar that frees up a slot — one icon fewer.

  The icon appears to the left of all the others. To put it right next to the battery
  (or where the battery used to be), drag it while holding Cmd — you can drag it into
  the system part of the menu bar, and the spot is remembered. Placing it there
  programmatically is impossible: in macOS 27 only dragging sets icon positions.


CHATGPT IN SIRI
---------------
  ChatGPT in Siri works in any system language, but turning the extension on and
  signing in is ONLY possible while the system is in English: in other languages
  macOS considers ChatGPT unavailable and the sign-in is dropped after a second.

  While ChatGPT is not connected, the menu has "Connect ChatGPT…". It restarts the
  Mac the same way but stops in English and opens Siri settings: turn the extension
  on, press Sign In, sign in to your account and press Done — your language comes
  back. The window waits 15 minutes, after which the language returns by itself. Once
  ChatGPT is connected, the item disappears.

  IMPORTANT: after your language comes back, the settings will again show a "Sign In…"
  button and "ChatGPT unavailable". That's not true — ChatGPT works under your
  account. Do NOT press "Sign In…" and don't touch the ChatGPT switch in your own
  language.

  Using it: pick ChatGPT in the Siri window or start your question with "Ask
  ChatGPT…". Questions about Siri and Apple itself are kept by Siri — ask those in the
  ChatGPT app. ChatGPT answers are labelled "ChatGPT"; without the label, Siri
  answered.


THIS IS NORMAL
--------------
  • The lock screen and the Apple menu windows ("Restart?", "Shut Down?") stay in
    English: they are drawn by the login session, which started in English.
  • The "Background activity" notification after a restart is AI Restart restoring
    your language.
  • On a Mac with a Touch Bar the Siri icon in it stays the old one — Apple never
    made a new one.
  • Siri answers "I can't help with that" to profanity and ambiguous words — that's
    an Apple filter and it cannot be turned off. Rephrase.


IF SOMETHING WENT WRONG
-----------------------
  • Siri AI didn't turn on after the restart → restart from here once more. Your
    language comes back either way, and the notification says that Siri AI did not
    turn on this boot.
  • The system is still in English more than 5 minutes after login → menu bar icon →
    "Restore Language…".
  • Logs: ~/Library/Application Support/AIRestart/login.log and errors.log. They are
    in Russian — attach them as they are when you report a problem.


UNINSTALLING
------------
  Menu bar icon → Settings → "Uninstall AI Restart". Your language is restored, the
  login item is removed, settings and logs are erased. Then drag the app from
  Applications to the Trash.
